AI Saves Advisors Time. They’re Quitting It Anyway.

Morningstar just published its 2026 “Investor Perspectives for Advisors” report — 501 advisors surveyed in July and August. It contains one of the strangest findings I’ve seen in any advisor technology survey:

AI use among advisors dropped from 33% to 20% in a single year — even as 48% of advisors say the technology has saved them time.

Read that again. Nearly half say it works. A fifth still use it. People are walking away from a tool that does what it promised. That shouldn’t happen — unless the problem was never the tool.

The numbers

  • 33% of advisors used AI in 2025. In 2026: 20%.
  • 48% say AI has significantly or somewhat saved them time day to day.
  • 56% cite too much administrative or operational work as a top barrier to delivering the service they want.
  • Where it’s used: summarizing meetings and drafting emails (43%), brainstorming (36%), research and due diligence (35%).
  • 57% say AI’s most positive impact is in client communications — summarizing notes, drafting follow-ups.
  • Only 18% rate AI tools as highly reliable for investment recommendations.

Why quit something that saves you time?

Morningstar’s Joe Agostinelli gave the quote that explains the whole report: “AI is helping advisors run faster, but the treadmill is speeding up, too.” More documentation, faster service expectations, more client questions — AI is absorbing complexity rather than simply freeing up time.

That’s the paradox resolved. The hours AI saves don’t pile up as free time. They get reabsorbed immediately — by the next client question, the next documentation demand, the next expectation that arrived faster because everything else got faster too. You run faster, the treadmill speeds up, and at the end of the quarter you’re exactly as buried as before. Eventually, some advisors look at that and conclude the tool isn’t worth it.

But notice what they’re actually concluding. They’re not saying AI failed. They’re saying the time it saved had nowhere to go.

The operations read

This is the same story I’ve been writing about from the other direction. Saved time is only useful if your practice has somewhere to put it. In a firm with documented workflows, an extra hour goes to client engagement or business development — the things 61% of advisors in the Federated Hermes survey said they wanted AI to free them up for. In a firm running on memory and inbox archaeology, an extra hour dissolves into the next fire. Same tool, same hour saved, completely different outcome.

The advisors quitting AI aren’t wrong about their experience. They’re wrong about the diagnosis. The tool didn’t disappoint them — their operating system couldn’t hold what the tool delivered. You can’t store water in a sieve and then blame the faucet.

What this means for your practice

  • Before you judge the tool, audit the container. If AI saved you five hours a month and you can’t point to where those hours went, the problem isn’t the AI. Track where saved time actually lands for one month.
  • Give saved time a job description. Decide in advance what reclaimed hours are for — follow-up, prospecting, planning — and protect them like client appointments. Unassigned time always gets eaten by urgency.
  • Fix the reabsorption points. The 56% drowning in admin work are describing workflows, not a technology gap. Document the three processes eating the most hours before buying another tool.
  • Measure outcomes, not hours. “Saved time” that evaporates is a vanity metric. Track what the saved time produced — follow-ups completed, reviews done early, prospects contacted.

AI adoption will come back. The tools keep getting better, and the economics are too obvious to ignore permanently. But the firms that benefit won’t be the ones that adopt earliest — they’ll be the ones whose operations can actually hold the time the tools give them. Build the container first. Then turn on the faucet.

Source: Morningstar 2026 “Investor Perspectives for Advisors” (501 advisors, surveyed July–August 2026), via FA Magazine, published October 8, 2026.
