Your Follow-Up System Is Leaking Clients

Here’s an uncomfortable truth about most lost prospects: they weren’t lost to a competitor. They were lost to silence.

Nobody sat down, compared you to another advisor, and chose the other one. They just… forgot. Life got busy. Your name stopped coming up. And when they were finally ready to move, they called whoever had emailed them last month.

The leaks are almost always in the same three places.

Leak #1: No next action at the point of contact

Every conversation with a prospect should end with a scheduled next step — a call on the calendar, a document you’re sending Thursday, something. “I’ll follow up soon” is not a next action; it’s a wish. Wishes don’t survive the weekend.

Leak #2: The post-meeting void

The 48 hours after a first meeting are when a prospect’s enthusiasm is highest — and when most advisors go quiet “because I don’t want to seem pushy.” A short recap email within 24 hours isn’t pushy. It’s professional. Silence is what actually reads as disinterest.

Leak #3: The dormant pile

Every practice has a list of prospects who went quiet 30, 60, 90 days ago. Most advisors never touch them again — which is strange, because these are the warmest leads you’ll ever have. They already know your name. A simple “still thinking about this?” revival every quarter converts a shocking number of them.

The fix is a rhythm, not a personality trait

Good follow-up isn’t about being “a natural salesperson.” It’s a weekly discipline: every Friday, pull up every prospect without a next action and give each one a date. Thirty minutes. That’s the whole system. The advisors who do it stop leaking. The ones who don’t keep blaming the market.
