How to Keep Your CRM Clean

Most firms don’t have a CRM problem. They have a CRM maintenance problem.

Data rots quietly. Duplicates pile up. Fields go stale. Pipeline stages stop meaning anything — that “proposal sent” stage has held the same three prospects since March. Within a year, nobody trusts the system, so nobody uses it, so the data gets worse. It’s a slow spiral, and the fix isn’t a new CRM. It’s a rhythm.

Daily: five minutes

Log every interaction the day it happens. Every new record gets three things: a status, an owner, and a next action with a date. No exceptions. This is the habit everything else depends on — a CRM updated “tomorrow” is a CRM with fiction in it.

Weekly: fifteen minutes

Merge duplicates. Close out dead prospects — the ones who went quiet two months ago aren’t “warm,” they’re gone, and keeping them in the pipeline just lies to you about your numbers. Fill in records missing key fields.

Monthly: thirty minutes

Review your pipeline stages. If a stage hasn’t moved a prospect in 90 days, it doesn’t mean anything — retire it or redefine it. Spot-check ten random records for accuracy. You’ll be amazed what you find.

Quarterly: one hour

Archive what’s past your retention window. Rename the fields that confuse the team — if two people interpret a field differently, the data in it is meaningless. Confirm your integrations still sync; they break silently.

A CRM nobody trusts is just an expensive address book. Fifteen minutes a week keeps it the source of truth.
