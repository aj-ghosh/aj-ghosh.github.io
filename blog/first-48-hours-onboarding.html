The First 48 Hours: Why Onboarding Is Won or Lost Early

Your new client just signed. They’re excited — and quietly nervous. They’ve trusted you with their money, and now they’re watching to see if that trust was well placed.

They won’t judge you on your investment philosophy in week one. They’ll judge you on something much simpler: did anything happen after I signed?

Hour 0–48: the welcome

Within two days, the client should receive three things: a genuine welcome, a plain-English explanation of what happens next, and one clear action for them. That’s it. But most practices manage zero of the three — the paperwork goes into a queue, and the client hears nothing until someone needs a signature. Silence in week one reads as disorganization, no matter how good the advice is.

The handoff is where it breaks

The most common onboarding failure isn’t a bad process — it’s no handoff at all. The advisor who closed the client holds everything in their head, and the person doing the onboarding is working from fragments. A written handoff checklist — what was promised, what was discussed, what the client cares about — takes ten minutes and prevents the “wait, nobody told me that” conversation.

Transfers need a watcher

Account transfers stall. Everyone knows this. What separates good onboarding from bad is whether anyone notices. Every transfer gets a task with a follow-up date in the CRM — not “check occasionally,” a date. When the date arrives and the transfer hasn’t moved, someone acts. That’s the whole difference between a six-week transfer and a six-day one.

Early organization compounds

Here’s what advisors miss: the client is forming their story about you right now. “They had it handled from day one” becomes the lens for everything after — the reviews feel thorough, the fees feel justified, the referrals come easier. Or the opposite. The first 48 hours don’t just start the relationship. They set its ceiling.
