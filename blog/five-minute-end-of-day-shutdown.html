The 5-Minute End-of-Day Shutdown

The most expensive operations failures don’t happen in big moments. They happen in the gaps between days — the follow-up nobody scheduled, the task nobody assigned, the ball nobody knew was in their court.

A five-minute shutdown closes those gaps before they open. Before you log off, answer three questions.

1. What’s due tomorrow, and who owns it?

If the answer is unclear for any item, assign it now — while it’s fresh, not at 9am tomorrow when you’re reconstructing yesterday from memory.

2. Did every interaction today get logged?

Calls, emails, meetings — if it’s not in the CRM, it didn’t happen. Log it now; tomorrow you’ll forget the details, and next week you’ll forget it happened at all.

3. Is anything stuck?

Anything waiting on someone else for more than a couple of days needs a nudge or an escalation — not another day of silence. Silence is how “waiting on the custodian” becomes “we lost the transfer.”

Five minutes. Three questions. It’s the cheapest operations system ever built, and most practices don’t have it.
