How it works

Build it once. Keep it running monthly.

You build a system that helps the practice manage its opportunities and responsibilities — then you help the team keep that system working. The monthly meeting is part of the service, not the entire product.

Phase A — Pipeline Build

About four weeks · $2,500. A defined implementation project for a practice using an existing CRM. Begins once access and approvals are secured.

  1. Process review

    We map how prospect follow-up, new business, and client service actually work today — where things stall, who owns what, and what your CRM currently captures versus what it should.

  2. Workflow configuration

    Up to three agreed workflows get built inside your CRM: stages, next-action rules, deadlines, ownership, task templates, and a pipeline dashboard. We use your existing approved tools — no new software to buy.

  3. Documentation & training

    Responsibility docs, standard procedures, and hands-on training for you and your assistant or team — so the system doesn't depend on any one person's memory.

  4. Live test & sign-off

    We run representative real work through the system together. The acceptance test is simple: your team can use the system — not merely that it was built.

Phase B — Monthly Operations Support

$750/mo · ~4 hrs/month. Three visible outputs every month. This is what the retainer buys — concrete, reviewable, and tied to how the practice actually runs.

Output 1

Monthly scorecard

Overdue follow-ups, opportunities without next actions, aging onboarding items, and upcoming client-service obligations — the exceptions that need a decision, not a data dump.

Output 2

Review meeting

A 45–60 minute session with you and your assistant: discuss the exceptions, agree on priorities, assign responsibilities with dates.

Output 3

Limited upkeep

Refine an existing workflow, update a template, resolve a process issue, or run a short training refresher — keeping the system aligned as the practice changes.

What monthly support is not: daily data entry, prospect calling, appointment setting, assistant recruiting, or major new implementations. Your team remains responsible for completing its daily tasks. Some clients eventually need only quarterly reviews — that's a legitimate outcome, and the model supports it.

Ground rules

You own your system

Your data stays in your CRM, under your accounts, with only the permissions needed. The workflows are documented so your team can run them without me. Renewal should be earned by continuing value — reporting, adjustments, oversight — not by dependency.

Compliance comes first

Access, configuration changes, and data handling are confirmed with the appropriate compliance contact before work begins. For advisory firms, service-provider access follows the firm's own oversight requirements.
