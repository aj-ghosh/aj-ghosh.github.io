Monthly Budget Worksheet

A simple monthly budget, organized the way the 50/30/20 guideline sees it: what you need, what you want, and what you keep. Enter the numbers and the totals adjust as you type. Advisors — this is built to be shared with clients; households — it's built to be finished in one sitting.

Private by design: the worksheet runs entirely in your browser. Nothing you type is uploaded, saved, or sent anywhere.

The 50/30/20 split is a starting point, not a verdict. In high-cost housing markets, needs alone can run past 50% — the value of the worksheet is seeing your actual split clearly, then moving it a few points at a time.

Before you start

Gather four things first

1. A recent pay stub

Budgets built on salary figures fail quietly. Budgets built on take-home pay — what actually arrives each month — hold up. If income varies, use a conservative recent month.

2. Last month's statements

Fixed costs are easy to list from memory. Variable costs are not. One pass through last month's bank and card statements turns guesses into numbers — groceries, fuel, subscriptions, the small stuff that adds up.

3. Every debt, with its minimum

Minimum payments on cards and loans belong in Needs — they're obligations. Anything you pay above the minimum belongs in Savings & debt payoff, because that's the part that's actually moving you forward.

4. Ten honest minutes

The first pass doesn't need to be perfect; it needs to be honest. Fill in what you know, estimate the rest, and let the percentages show you where the conversation is. You can refine it next month.
